# Candidate abstract — v0.16 evidence freeze

Unsupervised feature selection is difficult when relevance must be assessed without labels and when redundant variables can preserve the same sample structure. We investigate Geometry-Supported Residual Feature Selection (GSR-FS), a label-blind framework that represents each feature through its additive contribution to pairwise squared-distance geometry. Feature relevance is permutation-calibrated, concentrated geometry is downweighted using Sample-Geometric Coverage, and greedy selection penalizes geometry already explainable by non-negative combinations of previously selected feature-geometry profiles. The residual exponent was frozen at 0.5 before later external evidence. Automatic selection uses a max-null permutation threshold and can return an empty set. In 200 independent-null simulations, the empirical family-wise false-selection rate was 6.5% (13/200) at nominal alpha=0.05, while 30/30 redundant-signal simulations recovered exactly two latent signal groups with mean group coverage and relevant-feature precision of 1.00 and zero redundancy. In a heterogeneous source-audited panel of 30 real classification datasets, 29 supported complete comparison with canonical scikit-feature implementations of Laplacian Score, SPEC, and MCFS plus MaxVariance. GSR-FS achieved mean balanced accuracy 0.6031 and mean rank 2.862, compared with 0.6019 and 2.690 for SPEC. The Friedman test was not significant (p=0.488), and no GSR-FS versus canonical comparison remained significant after Holm correction. These results support GSR-FS as a competitive label-blind structural feature-selection framework, but not as a statistically established replacement for existing methods. Formal OpenML-CC18 and recent-author FGMRW-UFS validation remain predeclared confirmatory analyses.
